# Problem Statement

## Background

The Aral Sea was once the fourth-largest inland lake in the world. Beginning in the 1960s, large-scale irrigation projects diverted water from its two primary sources, the Amu Darya and Syr Darya rivers, to support agricultural production throughout Central Asia.

Over the following decades, these diversions dramatically reduced the amount of freshwater reaching the Aral Sea. As a result, much of the lake disappeared, fisheries collapsed, local economies suffered, and millions of acres of former seabed became a source of salt and dust storms.

While restoration efforts in northern Kazakhstan have demonstrated that recovery is possible, much of the basin continues to experience significant water losses before river water reaches the sea.

---

## The Problem

Large irrigation systems contain thousands of miles of canals constructed over many decades. Many of these canals experience water loss through:

- Seepage into surrounding soil
- Evaporation
- Aging infrastructure
- Inefficient water management
- Limited monitoring and maintenance

Many governments and water managers lack inexpensive, continuously updated information that identifies where repairs will produce the greatest improvement in water conservation.

Although satellite imagery, remote sensing, and artificial intelligence have advanced significantly, these technologies are not yet widely integrated into an open, accessible platform for prioritizing irrigation infrastructure improvements.

---

## Opportunity

Modern satellite imagery is freely available from multiple international programs. Combined with geographic information systems (GIS), weather data, and machine learning, these datasets can help identify areas where irrigation infrastructure may be losing significant amounts of water.

Providing better information can help engineers, researchers, and governments prioritize maintenance projects, improve irrigation efficiency, and support long-term restoration of river systems and wetlands.

---

## Proposed Solution

OpenCanal is an open-source platform designed to support irrigation infrastructure analysis through geospatial technology.

The project will combine:

- Satellite imagery
- GIS mapping
- Open environmental datasets
- Artificial intelligence
- Historical imagery
- Engineering reports

The objective is to provide decision-support tools that help identify canal segments where maintenance or modernization could improve water efficiency.

OpenCanal is intended to complement existing engineering and restoration efforts by providing transparent, data-driven analysis that can be shared freely with researchers, governments, universities, and nonprofit organizations.

---

## Initial Scope

Version 0.1 of OpenCanal will focus on the Aral Sea Basin.

Initial objectives include:

- Mapping major irrigation canals.
- Displaying publicly available satellite imagery.
- Collecting historical environmental data.
- Documenting existing restoration projects.
- Building a framework for future water-loss analysis.

Future versions may incorporate automated change detection, vegetation analysis, canal monitoring, machine learning models, and decision-support tools for infrastructure planning.

---

## Vision

OpenCanal seeks to become an open-source platform that enables engineers, scientists, governments, and communities to make better decisions about irrigation infrastructure through transparent data, modern geospatial technologies, and collaborative software development.

By improving the efficiency of irrigation systems, OpenCanal aims to contribute to healthier river systems, improved water security, and long-term ecosystem restoration.
